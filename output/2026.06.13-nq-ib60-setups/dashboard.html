IB definition

the initial balance (IB) is the high and low printed from 9:30 am to 10:30 am ET — the first 60 minutes of RTH. it defines the early-session range that the rest of the day either respects or breaks.

three setups

setup A — read the IB formation in real time: which side prints first predicts the post-IB break direction (63–81%). setup B — after 10:30, trade the IB break with extension targets. setup C — fade the failed IB break (double-break sessions).

ES as confirmation

ES and NQ run identical IB patterns. when ES breaks an IB extreme first, use it as the NQ entry trigger — ES leading NQ on the same IB break is the highest-confidence version of any of the three setups.

how to use ES as the NQ entry trigger

ES and NQ run in near-lockstep on IB breaks. when ES cleanly breaks the IBH or IBL while NQ is still near its IB level, that divergence is the signal. NQ will typically follow within minutes.

the mechanism: ES breaking its IB confirms institutional commitment to the direction. a solo NQ break with ES still inside is a fakeout candidate (part of the 28% of NQ breakouts that fail to reach L1).

entry protocol for ES-led IB break:

  • ES breaks IBH (or IBL) cleanly with momentum
  • NQ is still at or near its own IB level
  • enter NQ in the same direction as ES on the first NQ pullback to the IB level
  • stop: back inside NQ IB by more than 0.1%. target: NQ L1 (58% breakout / 72% breakdown)

setup C — break-fail + reversion trade plan

how to recognize and trade it
10:30
IB fully defined. price breaks one side — say the IBH breaks (bullish move).
10:30–11:00
failure signal: the breakout stalls and reverses back inside the IB within 1–3 candles. price closes back below IBH on a full 5-min candle. this is the failed break.
11:00
entry: short (fade the failed breakout) when price closes back below IBH. stop above IBH. target: IBL first, then L1 extension below IBL (72% of breakdowns reach L1 once IBL breaks).
11:00+
confirmation: ES also fading back from its IBH. if ES is holding above its IBH while NQ fails, avoid — this may be NQ catching up to ES, not a true failure.
why this setup works

the 60min IB only generates a double-break on 14% of sessions. the single-break rate (80%) means the first IB break has strong trend-day odds — but when the first break IS failing, the aggressive sellers (or buyers) who caused the break are now trapped.

a failed IBH break = longs are trapped above the IB. when price closes back below IBH, those longs exit simultaneously, creating the fuel for the reversion to IBL and beyond.

the setup C R:R is typically superior to setup B because you're entering after a confirmed failure rather than chasing momentum:

  • entry at/near IBH (the failed breakout level)
  • stop just above IBH — tight
  • target IBL (the full IB range away) — then L1 beyond that

filter: require ES to also fail its IBH within the same window. if only NQ fails and ES holds, this is a divergence trade, not a setup C — different playbook.
