The regime: how far days travel beyond their first hour

I tested about 50 daily measures of volatility, trend and recent opening-range behaviour against trade outcomes. One held up in every year: the median, over the last 20 full sessions, of the day's range divided by its IB range. I call it the regime reading. Near 1.3–1.5, days stay close to the first hour and fades at the IB edges work. Above about 1.7, days expand and fades fail.

Your charts are back-adjusted

Your chart shows about 17,470 for November 1 2023, when the contract traded near 14,700. Back-adjustment lifts older prices by a little over 1% per quarterly roll, so rules written as a percent of price (the 0.9% large-IB skip, the 0.40–0.60% sizing band) see older days as smaller than they were. Live bars are unaffected. It is why Deep Backtesting, which uses real prices, found 154 trades where your chart found 151, and it means a chart backtest can shift by a few trades after each roll.

  • TradingView's compiler accepted the overnight script and the new v3.1 code, and its regime reading matched mine on five dates to four decimals.
